## Current Research Themes

### 1. Elucidating the molecular evolutionary mechanisms underlying seasonal adaptation in temperate-zone animals

The Earth hosts a wide variety of environments, each inhabited by animals adapted to its specific conditions. In temperate regions such as Japan, where distinct **"seasons"** exist, resident animals are exposed to dramatic changes in day length (photoperiod) and temperature.

Animals living in temperate zones have acquired the ability to precisely sense day length and adjust their physiology and behavior accordingly (a phenomenon known as photoperiodism), as well as the ability to tolerate a wide range of temperatures. However, the molecular evolutionary mechanisms underlying these abilities remain unclear. We are addressing this question using medaka (*Oryzias latipes*) and its related species as model organisms. Medaka, which are widely distributed across Japan, do not lay eggs under short-day conditions but lay eggs almost daily under long-day conditions. They also tolerate the scorching heat of summer and the bitter cold of winter with ease. In contrast, closely related species inhabiting tropical regions lack these abilities. By conducting comparative analyses between medaka and its relatives that have adapted to diverse environments, we aim to uncover the molecular evolutionary mechanisms underlying seasonal adaptability.

#### 1-1. Research on the novel photoperiodic gene *phod1*

Animals sense seasonal change by using day length (photoperiod) as a cue. We discovered a novel gene, "*photoperiod decoder 1*" (*phod1*), that decodes this day-length information within the body. *phod1* shows a unique expression pattern with two daily peaks, in the morning and evening, but only under long-day conditions. We are working to clarify what function *phod1* performs in regulating seasonal responses, as well as the mechanism that generates this distinctive bimodal expression profile.

#### 1-2. What underlies the remarkable ability to adapt to temperature?

Medaka can flexibly adjust their physiological functions in response to seasonal changes in water temperature. By combining omics analyses (transcriptomics, proteomics, chromatin structure analysis, etc.) across multiple tissues, including the brain, liver, and heart, with functional analyses using genome editing, we aim to reveal, from multiple angles, the sophisticated mechanisms by which animals adapt to cold and heat.

## Research Approaches

Our group combines the following approaches in our research.

* **Omics analysis**: Comprehensive analysis using next-generation sequencing. We have also recently begun exploring proteomics.
* **Comparative genomics and transcriptomics**: Comparing genome sequences and gene expression dynamics among medaka-related species adapted to different environments.
* **Functional analysis using genome editing**: Analyzing the function of candidate genes in living medaka using tools such as the CRISPR/Cas9 system.
* **Histological analysis**: Analyzing the function of candidate genes within medaka tissues.
* **Cell-based assays**: Analyzing the function of candidate genes in medaka cells.
* **Behavioral analysis**: Quantifying behavior using automated behavior-tracking systems.
* **Fieldwork**: Observation and measurement in natural habitats as needed.
